# Coupled Physics (workshopping)

A heat analysis and a stress analysis of the same geometry, solved together: the heat problem first, its temperature field handed to the stress problem, then the stress problem with that temperature as a load of its own. Both 2D or both 3D. A **Fluid Flow** analysis of the same dimension can be wired in as well: the air takes heat from the solid, its pressure loads the stress analysis, and the structure can move the flow (see "Fluid flow with it (2D)" and "Fluid flow with it (3D)" below). It is a block of the Workbench (`.gpx` project folder: `simulation.json` + `state.sim`), not a separate way of setting problems up.

## How it works

1. In the Workbench, wire a **Part or Assembly** block into a 2D or 3D **Thermal** block and into a 2D or 3D **Static Structural** block (the same dimension, the same geometry block), set each up in its own window as usual: the heat conditions; the supports and the material of the stress analysis, with its **thermal expansion coefficient** (1/K) and **reference temperature** (°C). The stress analysis needs supports but **no mechanical load**: the temperature is its load. A stress analysis may also have loads; they act together with the temperature.
2. Add a **Coupled Physics** block (Analysis Systems in the toolbox) and wire the Setup cell of the thermal block and the Setup cell of the structural block into its **Physics** cell. Refresh checks that there is one of each, the same dimension and the same geometry block, and says in plain words what is wrong when there is not.
3. **Update** (or Solve in the Coupled Physics window): the heat analysis is solved, every node of the stress mesh takes the temperature at its place in the heat mesh (the two analyses keep their own meshes; the temperature is **interpolated**, never extrapolated), and the stress analysis is solved with the thermal strain `eps0 = alpha (T - Tref)` of every element.
4. The window shows the temperature range and average, the largest displacement and von Mises stress, what was handed over (points, direct copy or interpolation, the range on both meshes) and four checks:
   - **Nothing extrapolated** (stops the result): no stress-mesh node lies outside the heat mesh;
   - **Every point found** (stops the result);
   - **Average temperature kept**: the average of the stress mesh differs from the average of the heat mesh by less than the tolerance (2 % of the temperature range by default; a box in the window);
   - **Stress solve used the field** (stops the result): the stress analysis reports the temperatures it was given.
   The temperature and stress pictures are in the Results pages of the two analyses; they hold this solve.

## Fluid flow with it (2D)

Wire a **2D Fluid Flow** block into the Physics cell next to the 2D Thermal block and / or the 2D Static Structural block of the same sketch (one Fluid Flow, one Thermal, one Static Structural at most). It gives three things; each is in the window as a row of checks with PASS or FAIL.

1. **Heat exchange (fluid <-> thermal).** In the Fluid Flow window tick *Include heat transfer*, give the inlet temperature and set a condition on every wall: *Fixed temperature*, *Heat flux* or **Wall of the thermal analysis (Coupled Physics)**. A wall of the thermal analysis takes its temperature from the solid and gives the heat flux back. The Coupled Physics solve then finds the temperature and the heat flux on those walls that the two analyses agree on: the flow is solved ONCE (its temperature is a passive scalar: constant properties, no buoyancy), and the solid and the temperature of the fluid are solved again and again with the wall temperature updated (a linearised Robin-Dirichlet Newton step with relaxation) until the heat flux on both sides of the wall differs by less than a tolerance (1 % by default, 25 passes at most, boxes in the window). Checks: **Flow settled** (the flow reached a steady state; when it did not, the result is marked FAIL and says it belongs to the last time step), **Heat exchange converged** (stops the result), **Heat leaving the solid = heat entering the fluid** (within 5 %; stops the result), **Every wall point found** (a wall point of one mesh lies within half an element of the wall of the other; stops the result), **Fluid energy balance** (the heat the walls give to the fluid is the heat the flow carries away; 5 %), **Temperature inside the given range** (the fluid temperature does not overshoot the wall and inlet temperatures by more than 2 % of their range). The thermal conditions the Thermal window had on a wall that is wetted by the fluid are replaced in the coupled solve (it says so); the other edges keep theirs.
2. **Pressure (fluid -> stress).** The pressure of the flow is carried to the edges of the stress analysis that are **unsupported** (a supported edge takes any load); on an edge as drawn, the pressure at every point of the wall of the flow is put on the same point of the stress edge, whatever the two meshes. Shear can be added (a box in the window, off by default: the pressure of a slow flow is tiny and the shear smaller). Check **Pressure force handed over**: the force the stress analysis carries is the force the fluid pushes with, within 2 % (a hard check: a pressure that is not handed over stops the result).
3. **The structure moves the flow (stress -> fluid), 2D, steady.** Tick *The structure moves the flow* (it needs a stress analysis wired in, and the automatic refinement of the Fluid Flow window off). The loop is: pass 1, the flow round the body as drawn and the stress analysis with its pressure (and temperature); the displacement of the outline of the body goes to the fluid window, which meshes the moved outline (gmsh splines through the displaced points; the box round it stays where it was) and solves the flow again; the stress analysis gets the new pressure; and so on with an Aitken relaxation (or a fixed factor) until the displacement from one pass to the next changes by less than a tolerance (1 % of the largest displacement by default; 12 passes at most; boxes in the window). The last pass is the answer, and with a heat exchange the heat loop runs inside every pass. Checks: **Moved flow converged** (stops the result; it also says in words when there is no steady state - the load grows faster than the structure resists - or when the loop was cut short), **Outline found on the stress mesh** (stops the result), **Displacement small enough for a linear stress analysis** (a soft check: 5 % of the size of the body). A body that does not move (a millionth of its size or less) stops at pass 1 with ONE flow solve, and says so.

### The 2D Fluid Flow window: temperature

Also usable without Coupled Physics: *Include heat transfer (temperature of the fluid)* in the window of the 2D Fluid Flow solves the temperature of the fluid with the flow (forced convection: the same quadratic elements as the flow with stabilisation, in one linear solve), shows the temperature field, the wall temperature and heat flux tables, and the heat transfer coefficient. The inlet has the inlet temperature, the outlet carries the temperature out, body walls are insulated unless they have a fixed temperature or a heat flux, and the energy balance (heat in through the walls = heat carried out) is in the report as a check.

### Known answers and tests

- A body that conducts so well that it is at one temperature (copper, 400 W/m/K, 20 mm, left edge held at 50 degC) has walls at 50 degC (the example finds 49.993 to 50.000); the heat that leaves the solid is the heat the fluid takes (0.6 % apart without a moved body, 1.9 % with one, see below), and the fluid energy balance is closed to better than 0.1 %.
- An exact solution of the heat loop on stand-in problems (a linear flow and solid with a known fixed point; a flow that is not given the walls; a sign flipped) and of the moving loop on stand-ins whose fixed point is known (`d = (K - A)^-1 f0`, every relaxation reaches it, a load that grows faster than the structure resists is refused) are in the **Self-check** of the window (26 rows).
- The meshing of a moved outline is compared with the same shape drawn directly (drag and pressure agree to 0.2 to 0.5 %): `test_cfd_moved_mesh.py`.

### What this does not do (said plainly)

- **No buoyancy**: the temperature does not change the flow (constant properties, forced convection only). A hot body in still air is not solved.
- **Laminar** flow and (optionally) the k-omega SST model of the 2D window; first-order accuracy near walls from the stabilisation. No mesh-refinement study is part of this version.
- **The pressures in the examples are tiny** (the example flow is air at 0.03 m/s: a few 1e-4 Pa), so they validate the hand-over, not a realistic load. The soft body of the example (E = 5e-8 MPa, on purpose) is there only so that the loop has something to do.
- **The structure moves the flow: in 2D, steady only** (the 3D version is in the next section); the stress analysis is **linear** (small displacements: the load of the moved flow is put on the edges as drawn, not on the moved ones), and the loop says when a displacement is more than 5 % of the body. The outline must not fold (the mesher refuses), automatic refinement of the fluid mesh must be off, a body with spline edges is not supported by the fluid mesher. With a heat exchange on a moved body, the temperature at every wall point is looked up on the reference thermal mesh, so the heat leaving the solid and the heat entering the fluid differ by about the stretch of the wall (1.9 % in the example, limit 5 %); a larger movement is flagged by the gap check.
- The fluid window keeps the moved mesh and the result of the last pass afterwards; a plain Solve there is refused in words (press Mesh to get the sketch as drawn again; the next coupled solve re-meshes as drawn).
- The setups stay in the windows of the analyses. The Parametric Study of the Fluid Flow window ignores the heat. The self-check cannot catch a fluid that returns the heat flux with the wrong sign (the energy balance and the equal-heat check do, in a real solve).
- Nothing here has been compared with commercial software or tried on real Windows: everything so far ran headless.

## Fluid flow with it (3D)

Wire a **3D Fluid Flow** block into the Physics cell next to the 3D Thermal block and / or the 3D Static Structural block of the same Part (one of each at most; a 2D analysis with a 3D flow is refused in words, as are two parts and two flows). The same three things as in 2D, on the wall NODES of the 3D flow mesh; each is a row of checks with PASS or FAIL in the window.

1. **Heat exchange (fluid <-> 3D Thermal).** In the 3D Fluid Flow window tick *Solve the temperature too*, give the inlet temperature, and add the condition **Wall of the thermal analysis** on the faces the fluid shares with the solid. The flow is solved ONCE (its temperature is a passive scalar); the solid and the temperature of the fluid are solved again with the wall temperature updated (a linearised Robin-Dirichlet loop: the heat transfer coefficient of every wall node comes from one isothermal pass of the fluid, and only sets how fast it converges). The two analyses mesh the wall differently, so every field that crosses is interpolated on the triangles of the wall of the other mesh (nearest triangle, non-negative weights); how far a point lay from them is counted and reported. Checks: **Heat exchange converged** (the flux the solid takes and the flux the fluid gives agree, weighed by the heat every wall node carries, within 1 %), **Heat leaving the solid = heat entering the fluid** (5 %, hard), **Every wall point found** (hard), **Fluid energy balance** (soft: see below), **Temperature inside the given range** (soft).
2. **Pressure (fluid -> 3D Static Structural).** The wall pressure of the flow (gauge, relative to the outlet; the friction of the wall is NOT included: the shear option is refused in 3D) is put on the nodes of the faces that are wetted and not held by a support, in the units of the stress analysis. The stress analysis is the same linear one. Checks: **Pressure force handed over** (the force the stress analysis carries against the force the fluid pushes with, 2 %, hard) and **Same walls in both analyses** (hard).
3. **The structure moves the flow (3D, steady).** Tick *The structure moves the flow* (it needs a stress analysis wired in). The displacement of the stress analysis moves the wall nodes of the air mesh; the other air nodes follow by a smoothing (Laplace, weighted by the element volumes) with the outer box held where it is. The air is not meshed again. The loop is an Aitken-relaxed fixed point on the displacement of the wall nodes, as in 2D. A movement that turns an element inside out stops the solve with a message (move less, or make the box larger). The heat that crosses a moved wall is handed over by the material point: the other analyses know the part as drawn, so a value is looked up where the point WAS, while the area and the force come from the moved mesh. Checks: **Moved flow converged** (hard), **Outline found on the stress mesh** (hard), and the heat and pressure checks of the last pass.

What was seen (headless, in the Linux sandbox; the Workbench example project holds it as **Air, heat and pressure 3D**: a 30 x 10 x 10 mm copper block, upstream end held at 70 degC, air at 0.06 m/s, elements of 2.5 mm at the part, stress analysis with a very soft material, E = 4e-7 MPa, on purpose):

- Walls from 69.85 to 70.00 degC against the known 70 degC (the block conducts so well it is at one temperature); the heat exchange converged in 1 pass; 0.559 W leave the solid and 0.558 W enter the fluid (0.25 % apart).
- The force the stress analysis carries is 1.78e-7 N against 1.80e-7 N of the fluid (0.36 % apart): tiny, because a slow laminar flow pushes very little.
- With the structure moving the flow: 6 passes, largest displacement 0.204 mm, the heat the fluid takes 0.557737 W against 0.557597 W without the movement, the known answer still holds.
- On stand-in analyses with exact answers (the **Self-check** of the window, 18 more rows): the loop reaches the known fixed point also when the fluid and the solid have different meshes and a fluid whose flux is not local to each node; a half-heat solid, a half force, walls that differ, a gap that is too large, a flow that is not settled and a loop that does not converge are all caught.

What it does not tell you, said plainly:

- **The fluid energy balance FAILS (soft) on this coarse mesh: about 21 % against a limit of 10 %**, and the temperature of the fluid undershoots the range of the wall and inlet temperatures by a little (soft, limit 5 % of the range). Both are first-order stabilisation on elements that are long for the thin layer that carries the heat; the window says COARSE and the result is not stopped, because the heat that crosses the wall is checked on its own by the two hard checks above. The heat flux of the walls depends on the fluid mesh; a finer mesh at the part changes the numbers.
- Under a moved flow the heat of the two analyses differs a little more (0.29 % in the example against 0.25 % without the movement; limit 5 %).
- The stress analysis is **linear** (small displacements), the load of the moved flow is put on the part as drawn, and the displacement handed to the flow is that of the wall nodes. The pressure has no friction.
- The pressures of the example are about 1e-7 N in all: they validate the hand-over, not a realistic load.
- One Fluid Flow, one Thermal and one Static Structural analysis at a time; one part (no assembly) for the 3D flow.
- Nothing here has been compared with commercial CFD or with a measurement, and nothing has run on real Windows: everything so far ran headless in a Linux sandbox.

## Thermal expansion in the analyses (also usable without Coupled Physics)

- A material has an expansion coefficient `alpha` (1/K) and the analysis a reference temperature (°C, the temperature at which the body is free of thermal stress). The library materials carry typical values (steel 12e-6, aluminium 23e-6, ...); the 2D and 3D Static Structural windows show both numbers in their Material section.
- 2D (plane stress, plane strain, axisymmetric) and 3D (tetrahedra and hex-dominant) take a temperature field as a load. Not in the nonlinear 2D solver (with the linear solver also ticked, the linear result is shown with a WARNING line; with the nonlinear solver alone the solve is refused) and not with the adaptive-mesh loops (2D and 3D refuse and say so): the temperature is never left out silently.

## Known answers

- Free bar, one end 100 °C and the other 20 °C, `alpha` 12e-6, reference 20 °C: mean temperature 60 °C, the far end grows `alpha (Tmean - Tref) L` = 0.048 mm for L = 100 mm, and the stress stays small.
- The same bar held at both ends: the stress along it is `-E alpha (Tmean - Tref)` (about -96 MPa for steel), and it moves `alpha (Tmax - Tmin) L / 8` = 0.012 mm at most.

## Testing it (and what to send back)

1. In the Workbench press **Load Example Project**. It holds **Bar heating** (3D Thermal), **Bar growth** (3D Static Structural: three symmetry planes, no load) and **Bar thermal stress** (Coupled Physics of the two), with a known answer of 0.048 mm for the far end of the bar; and **Square** (a 20 mm square), **Air flow** (2D Fluid Flow: air at 0.03 m/s, left wall at 50 degC, the other walls of the thermal analysis), **Square heat** (2D Thermal), **Square bending** (2D Static Structural: left edge held, no load, very soft on purpose) and **Air, heat and bending** (Coupled Physics of the three, the structure moving the flow), with a known answer of walls at 50 degC; and **Block**, **Block air flow** (3D Fluid Flow: air at 0.06 m/s along +x), **Block heat** (3D Thermal: copper, the upstream end at 70 degC), **Block pressure** (3D Static Structural, very soft on purpose) and **Air, heat and pressure 3D** (Coupled Physics of the three, the structure moving the flow), with a known answer of walls at 70 degC. Expect two soft FAIL rows in that last one on the coarse flow mesh (fluid energy balance, temperature range): they are reported, not hidden.
2. **Update Project** solves everything, the coupled blocks included (a few minutes in the test container for the whole example). Open **Bar thermal stress**, **Air, heat and bending** or **Air, heat and pressure 3D** (double-click its Results cell): the checks list shows PASS or FAIL for each check and for the known answer, with what was found beside what was expected. In the second one the report also lists the passes of the moving loop.
3. **Self-check** (in the window): solves problems with known answers on stand-in analyses (a linear temperature handed from one mesh to another, 2D and 3D, exact; no value outside the range of the heat problem; the average kept) and then tries the checks the wrong way round: a stress solve that is not given the field, a heat problem of 3D with a stress problem of 2D, a stress mesh far from the heat mesh. Each must be caught; each line shows PASS or FAIL. A self-check that cannot fail is worth nothing: `test_coupled_tab.py` breaks the hand-over on purpose and requires FAIL.
4. **Diagnostics report**: the members, the options, the last solve (times, transfers, checks, the known answer), the self-check, what the two analyses said last and what this window said (errors in full). It is saved as `diagnostics.txt` in the project folder every time it opens. Send it when something fails, is slow or looks wrong.

## Not in this version (said plainly)

- **Setups stay in the windows of the two analyses.** The Coupled Physics window drives them; there is no single window with tabs for heat and stress, and the Solve of a wired analysis is not locked (solving it alone is still possible).
- **One way only**: heat to stress. The stress does not change the temperature. A transient heat analysis is used at its final state.
- Parametric Study and Optimization of the Coupled Physics block: not yet (a study of one of the two analyses works as before).
- The temperature and stress pictures are in the two analyses' windows; the combined result shows numbers and tables.
- A stress analysis of a 3D body reproduces a uniform temperature stress closely; on the quadratic hex-dominant mesh a fixed end face shows a local end effect (its median stress is right, its maximum is not). The gradient is exact at the level of one element.
- No standalone uniform-temperature load (use the heat analysis with a fixed temperature on every face). `alpha` is in 1/K and the reference temperature in °C only; the Edit Material dialog for parts does not edit `alpha` (the Material section of the analysis does).
- The known-answer row is for 3D bodies; a 2D problem says so instead of showing PASS.
- Nothing here has been compared with commercial software or tried on real Windows: everything so far ran headless.
