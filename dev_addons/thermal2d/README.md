# 2D Thermal (workshopping)

Heat conduction in a flat plate, as another kind of Simulation project (`.gph`, like 2D FEA / 2D CFD / 3D FEA: sketch in, regions, conditions, mesh, solve, results, probes, report, parametric study).

What it solves (everything is stored and solved in SI and kelvin; File > Units decides only what is shown and how typed numbers are read):
- steady and transient conduction (theta method: Crank-Nicolson or backward Euler), quadratic (T6) or linear (T3) triangles;
- conditions: fixed temperature, heat flux, heat flow, convection (h and fluid temperature), radiation to the surroundings (emissivity and surroundings temperature; Newton iteration), and Insulated (no heat through the edge: an adiabatic wall or a line of symmetry; the same maths as a heat flux of zero, drawn as a hatch outside the edge). An edge with no condition at all is insulated too; a note under the conditions table counts those edges;
- a material for every region, all assigned in Material and analysis: a table of the regions with their material, and the box of the selected region to choose from the thermal library or type k, density and specific heat ("(custom values)"); "Use for all regions" copies one region's material to the others. Regions whose part has no thermal values carry the default material and are marked "choose a material" until you pick one. The part's own Edit Material dialog is not changed. Projects saved before this keep their results: the old single material is copied into the regions that had none;
- heat generation per region (W/m3, in Geometry next to the region list, where regions are also switched in or out of the model);
- optional heat loss from the two flat faces of a plate (convection and radiation);
- results: temperature, heat flux, heat through every condition, energy balance (in - out - stored), probes, a time slider for transient runs;
- heat flow (Results > Heat flow): moving tracer particles made and drawn like the tracers of the 2D CFD (700 particles, white trails and dark dots, the same pacing and lifetimes), carried along the heat flux; they start again at the edges where heat enters the body. Or arrows on a grid. Works for a steady result and for every picture of a transient run (also while Animate plays). Display only: the speed shows the flux compared with the strong flux of the picture, it is not a real speed. The exported animation file does not contain the tracers. Animate itself needs a transient run; a steady result says so and points to Heat flow. `FluxField.inlet_seeds(n)` (also used by the Viz tab of Coupled Physics) gives n starting points along the inlet edges, each standing for the same share of the heat that enters.

- units (File > Units > Heat transfer, `heatunits.py` in the program): a unit for every thermal quantity - temperature (K, degC, degF, degR), heat rate (W, kW, Btu/hr, cal/s, kcal/hr ...), heat flux, radiative heat flux, heat generation, conductivity, convection coefficient, density and specific heat - as one of nine well-known combinations (SI, metric with kW, metric small parts in mm, CGS with calories, metric heating with kcal/hr, US with Btu/hr, US with Btu in, US per second in degR) or Custom, one by one. The default "Follow the unit system" is what the tab did before: degC and W with the metric unit systems, degF and Btu with the US ones. Changing the heat units converts the numbers in the boxes (the same physical value; the model, mesh and result are SI and do not change) and redraws every label, the conditions table, the report, the legend and the study lists in them. A saved tab remembers the units of its boxes; a file saved before that was written in the units of its unit system. The report's table of heat through each condition has an average heat flux per condition (the radiative one in the radiative flux unit). The calorie and the Btu are the International Table ones (1 Btu/(lb F) = 1 cal/(g K) = 4186.8 J/(kg K)). The length unit stays with the unit system. Temperatures are absolute values; a unit that is "per degree" is per degree of difference. A parametric study keeps the numbers typed into its table in the units that were shown when they were typed: change the heat units, then look at the study again.

Not in this version: axisymmetric, contact resistance between regions, thermal stress (temperature feeding 2D FEA), view-factor radiation, temperature-dependent properties, automatic mesh refinement.
Material values are typical room-temperature numbers taken as constant: check them against your own source. Nothing here has been compared with commercial software or tried on real Windows.

Files: `thermal_fem.py` (maths, no Qt), `thermal_units.py` (the units of the tab; the tables are `heatunits.py` in the program), `thermal_mat.py`, `thermal_store.py` (saved tab in another unit system), `thermal_tab.py` (the tab), `thermal_flow.py` (tracers and arrows, no Qt), `thermal_study.py` (parametric studies), `thermal_ext.py` (registers the project kind).
Tests (repository root): `test_thermal_fem.py`, `test_thermal_tab.py`, `test_thermal_regions.py` (materials per region, Insulated), `test_thermal_flow.py` (heat flow), `test_thermal_units.py` (heat transfer units on the tab), `test_heat_units.py` (the tables, File > Units dialog and setting), `test_thermal_addon.py`.
